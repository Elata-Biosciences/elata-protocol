Elata Protocol 
Self‑contained smart contract upgrade plan (Base Sepolia first; mainnet‑ready)
This document is the updated, consolidated plan for all Solidity changes. It is intended for two uses:
* Engineering execution: you can paste sections into Cursor and implement contract-by-contract;
* Stakeholder alignment: it explains the design principles in plain language; then gets very specific about flows, roles, parameters, and tests.
The guiding theme is simple and old-fashioned: simplicity is security; revenue is only real if it ends in something you can spend.
________________


0) Scope and non-goals
Scope
* All contracts in elata-protocol that define:
   * app creation and app token launch;
   * bonding curve sale and graduation to Uniswap V2 liquidity;
   * protocol fee capture and distribution;
   * staking and reward distribution for app tokens and veELTA;
   * modules: tournaments; in‑app content; airdrops; referrals (if included).
Non-goals
* No “app status registry” or on-chain censorship; front end curation only.
* No per‑app on‑chain governance; app “governance” is roles + multisig, with off‑chain Snapshot voting as advisory.
* No complex token-voting mechanics (no ERC20Votes on app tokens); vote weight is computed off‑chain from on‑chain balances.
________________


1) The two invariants that matter
Invariant A: Treasury revenue must be spendable
Treasury revenue must end up in native USDC on Base; not primarily app tokens; not “maybe we sell later.”
If we do not enforce this; the protocol can appear “profitable” while being unable to pay for audits, ops, liquidity, or growth.
Invariant B: The system must remain auditable
No unbounded loops in any function that must succeed for funds to be claimable; no implicit state machines; no “magic balances.”
________________


2) The high-level mental model (general audience)
Elata is an “app token launchpad + fee router”:
1. Developers launch an app token through the protocol;
2. The token sells along a bonding curve for ELTA;
3. The app graduates to a Uniswap V2 pool (AppToken/ELTA); LP tokens are locked;
4. Trades on the DEX incur a transfer tax inside AppToken (only on LP interactions);
5. Those taxed app tokens are periodically swept; swapped into ELTA; and then a portion is settled into USDC for the protocol treasury;
6. The remainder is distributed to ecosystem stakeholders (app stakers; veELTA stakers; optionally creators; optionally referral rewards).
Two consequences fall out:
* If users trade and spend; the protocol earns revenue; and the treasury balance in USDC rises.
* If there is no usage; no on-chain design can save the business. The goal is simply to ensure that when usage occurs, it turns into real treasury assets automatically and safely.

3) Key decisions (locked)
3.1 App tokens are fee-on-transfer exactly in the “Virtuals-style” sense
* Tax applies only when from or to is an allowlisted LP/pair address (DEX buys and sells);
* Normal wallet-to-wallet transfers are not taxed;
* The set of taxable LP addresses is explicit (allowlist), not automatically detected.
This preserves usability while capturing DEX volume.
3.2 No on-chain governance for app tokens
* No per-app Governor; no on-chain proposals; no on-chain voting.
* App-level knobs are controlled by roles; with the team multisig as ultimate admin.
* Token-holder voting is supported via Snapshot; it is advisory; the multisig executes results.
This is the simplest secure structure that still gives you “DAO-like” coordination.
3.3 Team vesting starts immediately; uses OpenZeppelin standards
* Team allocation is held in an OZ-style vesting wallet from app creation time (T0);
* The vesting schedule begins at T0; a cliff may exist, but the schedule is defined at creation.
3.4 Fees distribute in daily windows
* Fees are accrued continuously but distributed in epochs (target: 1 day);
* Anyone can trigger epoch settlement; caller receives a small incentive;
* Incentive is paid in USDC; capped; only paid when meaningful work occurs.
3.5 Treasury settles into USDC only
* Settlement asset for treasury is native USDC on Base;
* The contracts treat USDC as the “cash register.”
3.6 App token supply is always fixed at 10,000,000
* Always 10,000,000; 18 decimals; minted at app creation.

This is the correct order; it minimizes refactors fighting each other.
P0; foundation (must ship before next meaningful testnet)
1. Remove LotPool completely;
2. Protocol governance uses veELTA; add Timelock for parameter changes;
3. Introduce ProtocolConfig with bounds for every tunable parameter;
4. Implement fee pipeline: FeeCollector + Swapper + daily settlement + treasury USDC vault;
5. Implement AppToken LP-keyed transfer tax; route tax to FeeCollector;

7. Team multisig + operational roles at app creation; vesting wallet and allocations;
8. External dependency plan: Uniswap V2 addresses configurable; Base Sepolia uses trusted deployments; Foundry local deploys its own V2 stack.

P2; only if demanded

14. USD-denominated app creation fee (oracle introduces risk; avoid unless needed);
15. Burns (optional; configurable; do not complicate P0).

5) Contract architecture (what exists after vNext)
Core protocol contracts
* ELTA (ERC-20); should remain “plain” for maximal compatibility
* veELTA (vote-escrow staking); provides voting power for protocol governance
* ElataGovernor (protocol governance)
* ElataTimelock (timelock for governance changes)
* ProtocolConfig (all fee rates and parameters; bounded; evented)
App creation and trading
* AppFactory (creates apps; app token; bonding curve; staking vault; vesting wallet; ecosystem vault)
* AppToken (ERC-20; fixed supply; LP-keyed transfer tax; AccessControl roles)
* AppBondingCurve (sale contract; reclaimability; graduation to Uniswap V2; fee capture)
* LPLocker (locks LP tokens; time-locked; no early exit)
Revenue pipeline
* FeeCollector (receives fee assets; app tokens; ELTA; module fees; keeps per-app accounting)
* FeeSwapper (swaps non-ELTA assets to ELTA; strictly bounded slippage; router allowlist)
* FeeManager (epoch accounting; splits; pushes rewards)
* TreasuryUSDCVault (custody; receives USDC; emits clean revenue events)
Stake and rewards
* AppStakingVault (per app; deposits app tokens; tracks stake; provides stakedBalanceOf)
* AppRewardsDistributor (per app; ELTA rewards to stakers; epoch-based deposits)
* veELTARewardsDistributor (global; ELTA rewards to veELTA stakers; epoch-based deposits)
Optional modules
* TournamentFactory / Tournament (Merkle payout)
* InAppContent721 (ERC-721)
* ContentStore (optional purchase logic)
* AirdropDistributor (Merkle claim for ecosystem pool)
* ReferralRegistry + ReferralDistributor (if enabled)

6) Roles model (app-level; minimal and explicit)
We keep “control” very simple:
AppToken roles
* DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE: team multisig (ultimate admin)
* APP_OPERATOR_ROLE: team members who can manage day-to-day parameters (within bounds)
* LP_MANAGER_ROLE: can add/remove taxable LP addresses (bounded; allowlist)
* FEE_EXEMPT_MANAGER_ROLE: can set exemptions (router; swapper; protocol contracts; within a strict allowlist)
Module roles (tournaments; content store)
* MODULE_ADMIN_ROLE: team multisig
* MODULE_OPERATOR_ROLE: team members; can publish Merkle roots; set pricing; etc.
Protocol-level roles remain governed by veELTA + timelock, not by app teams.

7) Economic flows (with concrete examples)
This section is deliberately explicit; if it is not legible to a non-engineer, the design is too complicated.
7.1 App creation fee
* Developer calls AppFactory.createApp(...)
* Pays appCreationFeeElta in ELTA; configurable in ProtocolConfig
* This fee routes directly into the fee pipeline; ultimately a portion settles to treasury USDC
Example defaults
* appCreationFeeElta: 110 ELTA (configurable)
* 100% of creation fees treated as protocol revenue (split like other fees)
7.2 Bonding curve trading fees
Bonding curve buys and sells should be the most reliable fee source; because they run through your contracts.
* User buys: pays ELTA; receives app tokens
* A configurable bondingCurveTradeTaxBps is taken in ELTA
* The ELTA tax is forwarded to FeeCollector under that app’s accounting bucket
Example
* user buys with 1,000 ELTA
* bondingCurveTradeTaxBps = 100 (1%)
* fee = 10 ELTA; net = 990 ELTA into curve mechanics
7.3 DEX trading fees via AppToken LP-keyed transfer tax
After graduation, the app token is traded on Uniswap V2.
* Whenever app tokens move into or out of an allowlisted LP, a tax is taken in app tokens;
* The tax is transferred to FeeCollector.
Example
* app token transfer tax = 100 bps (1%)
* user buys from LP and receives 10,000 app tokens
* tax = 100 app tokens
* user receives 9,900; FeeCollector receives 100
7.4 Daily settlement and distribution (the “number goes up” mechanism)
At most once per day per app (or per token bucket), anyone can call settleAppFees(appId).
Settlement does:
1. Swap accumulated app-token fees into ELTA (via Uniswap V2 router allowlist)
2. Split ELTA into buckets (percentages in ProtocolConfig):
   * app stakers rewards
   * veELTA rewards
   * creator/team share (optional; strongly recommended)
   * treasury share
   * referrals (optional; if enabled)
3. Treasury share of ELTA is swapped into USDC and deposited into TreasuryUSDCVault
4. Caller receives a small USDC incentive; capped; taken from the amount being settled
Example split (illustrative; tune later)
* app stakers: 45%
* veELTA: 15%
* creator/team: 10%
* treasury: 25%
* referrals: 5% (only if enabled)
If you want the treasury to grow faster, raise the treasury share; keep bounds.

8) Revenue pipeline design (detailed; Cursor-ready)
8.1 Design goals
* No idle balances without a permissionless sweep path
* No slippage-free swaps
* No reliance on a privileged keeper
* No unbounded loops over “all apps”
* Treasury ends in USDC
8.2 FeeCollector (what it does)
FeeCollector is the single “sink” for protocol fee assets.
Responsibilities:
* Receive:
   * taxed app tokens from AppToken transfers
   * ELTA fees from bonding curve and modules
   * optional: USDC fees from modules
* Maintain per-app accounting:
   * pendingAppTokenFees[appId][token]
   * pendingEltaFees[appId]
* Expose permissionless sweep(appId, token) which forwards assets to FeeSwapper or directly to FeeManager
Key design rule:
* FeeCollector must not attempt to process “all apps”; sweeps are always per-app.
8.3 FeeSwapper (safe swapping)
FeeSwapper holds non-ELTA assets and swaps them into ELTA.
Hard requirements:
* Router allowlist; set via protocol governance timelock
* Slippage bounds:
   * caller supplies minOut; and contract enforces it
   * additionally enforce maxSlippageBps relative to quoted output when feasible
* Uses Uniswap V2 router “supporting fee-on-transfer” functions when swapping app tokens
* Reentrancy guard
* Emits: tokenIn; amountIn; tokenOut; amountOut; appId
8.4 FeeManager (daily epochs; distribution)
FeeManager holds ELTA and handles daily distribution windows.
Per app:
* lastEpochClose[appId]
* pendingEltaToDistribute[appId]
Flow:
* depositEltaForApp(appId, amount) can be called by FeeSwapper or bonding curve
* closeEpoch(appId):
   * require block.timestamp >= lastEpochClose + epochLength (epochLength = 1 day)
   * compute splits; transfer:
      * ELTA to AppRewardsDistributor(appId)
      * ELTA to veELTARewardsDistributor
      * ELTA to creator/team (optional)
      * ELTA to TreasurySettlement path (swap ELTA→USDC; deposit to vault)
      * ELTA to referral pool (if enabled)
   * pay caller incentive in USDC (see below)
   * update lastEpochClose
8.5 TreasuryUSDCVault (simple custody; clean accounting)
This contract should be intentionally boring.
Responsibilities:
* Receive USDC from FeeManager settlement step
* Emit events:
   * TreasuryRevenue(appId, usdcAmount, epochId)
* Optionally restrict transfers:
   * only allow withdrawals to a treasury multisig
   * optionally time-lock large withdrawals off-chain via multisig policy; do not over-engineer on-chain
8.6 Caller incentive mechanics (USDC; capped; not gameable)
Goal: someone should be happy to press the “settle” button daily.
Mechanism:
* Incentive is paid only when usdcSettled >= minIncentiveThresholdUSDC
* Incentive is:
   * min(maxCallerIncentiveUSDC, usdcSettled * callerIncentiveBps / 10_000)
* Incentive comes out of the treasury settlement slice, not out of staker rewards
Recommended defaults:
* epochLength = 1 day
* callerIncentiveBps = 10 (0.10%)
* maxCallerIncentiveUSDC = 25 USDC
* minIncentiveThresholdUSDC = 100 USDC
These values make spam unprofitable; but make real settlements worthwhile.

9) Bonding curve changes (correctness and reclaimability)
Your bonding curve is the part that can destroy trust if it breaks. It must satisfy three properties:
1. It cannot lock funds indefinitely;
2. It can only graduate once;
3. It behaves safely under adversarial conditions (sandwiching; flash loans; reentrancy).
9.1 Explicit lifecycle states
Implement explicit states in AppBondingCurve:
* PENDING (created; not active yet)
* ACTIVE (buy/sell enabled)
* GRADUATED (LP created; curve closed)
* CANCELLED (creator cancels before activation)
* optionally FAILED (if you later implement refunds)
9.2 Activation window (optional but helpful)
A short activation delay reduces bot sniping and gives humans time to react.
* activationTime = now + activationDelay
* enforce no trading before activationTime
9.3 Deadline and forced graduation (no indefinite lock)
Add:
* deadline = activationTime + maxDuration
If deadline reached and not graduated:
* force graduation with whatever has been raised; do not attempt refunds in P0 (refunds are storage-heavy and increase attack surface)
9.4 Graduation steps (must execute exactly once)
At graduation:
* create Uniswap V2 pair (AppToken/ELTA) if not existing
* add liquidity using deterministic amounts
* lock LP tokens in LPLocker
* mark state GRADUATED
* permanently disable curve trading
9.5 Slippage protections
Any call that swaps or adds liquidity must accept minOut parameters and enforce them.
9.6 Fee routing
Bonding curve trade fees are in ELTA; they should flow to FeeCollector/FeeManager; not sit inside the curve.
If you want daily windows:
* curve can accumulate fees and expose sweepFees(); permissionless; forwards to FeeCollector

10) App token design (10M supply; vesting; LP tax)
10.1 Supply and minting
* Total supply: exactly 10,000,000 * 10^18
* Minted once at creation; no minting after
10.2 Allocation buckets (recommended structure)
At creation, AppFactory mints supply into buckets:
1. Community Sale / Liquidity Bucket
   * minted to the bonding curve contract
   * used for bonding curve distribution and LP seeding
2. Team Vesting Bucket
   * minted to an OZ-style vesting wallet
   * beneficiary is the team multisig
   * schedule starts immediately at creation (T0)
3. Ecosystem / Developer Airdrop Bucket
   * minted to an AppEcosystemVault (simple custody)
   * distributed via Merkle airdrops and developer incentives
4. Optional: Protocol Reserve Bucket
   * if you want it; otherwise omit; keep it simple
You will need to pick exact percentages; the protocol can enforce bounds. A reasonable starting point:
* 50% community bucket (5,000,000)
* 25% team vesting (2,500,000)
* 25% ecosystem pool (2,500,000)
If you want a “liquid airdrop pool,” take it from the ecosystem bucket.
10.3 LP-keyed transfer tax (exact semantics)
Inside AppToken _transfer(from, to, amount):
* if neither side is exempt; and (isLiquidityPool[from] || isLiquidityPool[to]) then tax
* tax amount is sent to FeeCollector
* remainder is transferred to recipient
Critical details:
* The router must be in the exemption list, or tax swaps will tax themselves
* LP addresses must be allowlisted; you do not auto-detect them
* Tax bps is bounded in ProtocolConfig; e.g., 0–200 bps
10.4 No ERC20Votes on app tokens
AppToken remains a plain ERC-20 + AccessControl; no vote checkpoints.

11) Teams and vesting (OpenZeppelin; simple and safe)
11.1 Team multisig is the ultimate admin
At app creation:
* teamTreasury (multisig) becomes DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE for app contracts
* operational roles are granted to provided operators[]
11.2 Vesting wallet design
Use an OpenZeppelin-style vesting wallet pattern; either:
* OZ VestingWallet; or
* a thin wrapper AppVestingWallet that adds:
   * metadata (appId; token address; beneficiary)
   * events
   * optional: the ability to update beneficiary only via multisig (rarely needed)
Default schedule:
* start: immediately at app creation time
* cliff: 3 months
* duration: 24 months linear vest after cliff
This is easy to reason about and widely understood.
11.3 “Do vested creators get fees?”
Yes; but do not overcomplicate staking mechanics.
The simplest model:
* creators receive a creator/team fee share paid directly to team multisig (in ELTA)
* community stakers receive the app staker share via staking vault
* the vesting wallet does not need to participate in staking to earn creator fees
This preserves incentives without building complicated “vesting counts as stake” logic.

12) Off-chain voting plan (Snapshot) that reconciles liquid, staked, and vesting
You want token-holder voting; but you do not want on-chain voting contracts. That is the correct instinct.
12.1 What Snapshot needs
Snapshot can compute voting power by reading chain state; it does not require ERC20Votes.
For each app space, define vote power as:
VotingPower(addr) = liquidBalance(addr) + stakedBalance(addr) + attributedVestingBalance(addr)
Where:
* liquidBalance(addr) = AppToken.balanceOf(addr)
* stakedBalance(addr) = AppStakingVault.stakedBalanceOf(addr)
* attributedVestingBalance(addr):
   * for the team multisig only, add AppVestingWallet.totalTokenBalance() (or AppToken.balanceOf(vestingWallet))
   * this is attributed to the vesting wallet beneficiary; not to the vesting wallet address itself
This is off-chain code; it does not increase the protocol’s on-chain attack surface.
12.2 Enforcement policy
* Snapshot outcomes are advisory but documented as the “expected governance process.”
* Team multisig executes the chosen outcome by calling role-gated functions.
* In emergencies (exploits; broken modules), multisig can act unilaterally; document this openly.

14) Referrals (simple; spaced out; practical)
Referrals are easy to get wrong; the goal is “useful and honest,” not “clever.”
14.1 What referrals are for
* Reward users who bring in new users;
* Do not inflate; do not add new taxes;
* Pay from existing fee streams.
14.2 What referrals apply to (P1/P2 recommendation)
Do referrals only on protocol-routed actions:
* Bonding curve buys (best)
* In-app purchases (if you have a ContentStore)
* Tournament entries (if routed through protocol)
Do not attempt referrals on arbitrary DEX trades; you do not control the router and cannot reliably attach attribution.
14.3 Minimal referral mechanism
At bonding curve buy:
* buy(amountEltaIn, referrer)
Rules:
* referrer must not be zero; must not equal buyer
* if buyer already has a stored referrer for this app; ignore new referrer
* store referrerOf[buyer] = referrer once
When a fee is generated on a referred user’s action:
   * take referralBps out of the existing fee split
   * accrue it to pendingReferralRewards[referrer] (in ELTA)
   * pay out daily during epoch close; or via a claim function
14.4 Concrete example
Assume:
   * bonding curve trade fee = 1% (100 bps)
   * referral share = 5% of the fee (not 5% of the trade)
User A refers user B.
User B buys 1,000 ELTA of tokens.
   * fee = 10 ELTA
   * referral cut = 0.5 ELTA
   * the remaining 9.5 ELTA is split among app stakers; veELTA; treasury; creator
This is economically sane and non-extractive.
14.5 Abuse considerations
   * Sybil attacks; referral farming; self-referral
Mitigations:
   * one-time referrer binding
   * optional minimum purchase size to set referrer
   * optional “referrer must have staked X” requirement (later)
Keep P0 free of this complexity.

15) In-app content (renamed; ERC-721; optional royalties)
This is important, but not P0.
15.1 Naming
Do not call them NFTs in the product; call them “In-App Content” or “Digital Items.”
15.2 Contract approach
      * InAppContent721 implements ERC-721 metadata
      * Optional ERC-2981 royalties for secondary markets
      * Primary sales are enforced on-chain through ContentStore:
      * accept app token by default
      * optional support for ELTA and USDC later
15.3 Economics
Default for v1:
      * No burn by default
      * Route a configurable share of purchase revenue into the fee pipeline
      * Later you can introduce “burnBps” if you want deflationary pressure; bounded; per app
15.4 OpenSea readiness
Focus on:
      * stable URIs
      * metadata correctness
      * events emitted on mint and metadata updates
No special registration API should be assumed.

16) Tournaments (multi-currency; Merkle payouts)
Again; not P0; but plan it now so it fits the fee pipeline.
16.1 Basic v1 design
         * Tournament entry can be denominated in:
         * ELTA (default)
         * USDC (optional; useful for stable revenue)
         * app token (optional)
         * Tournament contract holds entry fees; takes rake; remainder is prize pool
         * Results are off-chain; payouts are committed on-chain via Merkle root
         * Users claim prizes with Merkle proofs
16.2 Admin controls
Tournament admin role:
         * set Merkle root once per contest window
         * can rotate to a new root if a mistake is made; with strict constraints and events
16.3 Fee routing
Tournament rake should be forwarded to FeeCollector; then distributed in daily windows.

18) Protocol governance (veELTA) and config changes
Even if you want app-level simplicity, protocol-level economics must be governed with restraint.
18.1 Switch protocol governance to veELTA
         * Voting power is derived from veELTA locks; with boosting logic
         * Ensure vote snapshots behave deterministically
18.2 Add Timelock
         * All changes to ProtocolConfig must go through timelock
         * Timelock delay is a social circuit breaker; it gives you time to react if governance is attacked
18.3 ProtocolConfig; what must be configurable
The following should not be hardcoded:
App creation
         * appCreationFeeElta
         * appCreationSeedElta (if used)
Bonding curve
         * bondingCurveTradeTaxBps
         * graduationTarget
         * lpLockDuration
         * maxDuration; activationDelay
         * XP gate parameters (if retained)
App token transfer tax
         * appTransferTaxBps (default)
         * max appTransferTaxBps cap
         * per-app override bounds (if you allow overrides)
Fee splits
         * appStakersBps
         * veELTABps
         * creatorBps
         * treasuryBps
         * referralBps (optional)
All bounded; must sum to 10,000.
Settlement
            * epochLength
            * router allowlist
            * maxSlippageBps
            * minSwapThresholds
            * caller incentive caps

21) Test plan (Foundry; invariants that prove correctness)
This is where you earn safety.
21.1 Fee pipeline invariants
            * Any taxed app token received by FeeCollector can be swept into ELTA
            * Daily settlement cannot be called twice for the same app within epoch
            * TreasuryUSDCVault receives USDC; not app tokens
            * Caller incentive never exceeds cap; only paid when settlement occurs
            * If swaps would exceed slippage bounds; settlement reverts safely; funds remain in FeeCollector/FeeSwapper
21.2 AppToken tax invariants
            * wallet→wallet transfer: no tax
            * wallet↔LP transfer: tax applied
            * router-exempt swaps do not self-tax
            * changing LP allowlist changes tax behavior immediately and only as intended
21.3 Bonding curve invariants
            * cannot trade before activation time
            * graduation executes exactly once
            * LP tokens are locked; cannot be withdrawn early
            * forced graduation after deadline works; no funds stuck
            * fee amounts are conserved and routed properly
21.4 Vesting invariants
            * team tokens vest correctly over time
            * cannot withdraw before cliff
            * beneficiary receives released tokens
            * if beneficiary changes (if allowed), only multisig can do it
21.5 Snapshot support tests (optional but valuable)
            * AppStakingVault.stakedBalanceOf matches internal accounting
            * vesting wallet balance is visible and attributable
